# About This Rewrite

This document is a reconstruction of the original campaign, not a claim that every event happened exactly as it is written here. It draws on the events recorded during play, the characters and stories that emerged from them, and the wants, ideals and intended development of the players behind those characters. Where the campaign's events and a player's intended character direction differed, this rewrite aims to bring those elements together into a coherent story while respecting both what happened at the table and what each character was meant to become.

The rewrite also adapts the campaign to the Shattered Lands setting. The original campaign was rooted in the Feyrune setting, but this version reworks its locations, history and wider context so that the events can be treated as part of Shattered Lands canon. This includes replacing or adapting setting-specific elements where necessary, while retaining the central events, relationships and themes that made the campaign meaningful.

It should therefore be read as the story of the campaign as remembered and reimagined, rather than a session-by-session transcript or a perfectly literal account. Its purpose is to preserve the shared adventure, give the characters' intended stories room to develop, and establish their place in the history of the Shattered Lands.

# Part One: Gleoda

## The Yawning Portal

Gleoda was a city where adventurers could disappear into the crowd. Its streets were crowded, its taverns loud, and beneath its respectable surface ran a criminal war between the Zhentarim and the Xanathar Guild. The party's first night in the city gave them no reason to believe they would become important to any of it. They were sitting in the Yawning Portal when the room erupted. Yagra Strongfist, a towering half-orc working for the Zhentarim, was already trading blows with Krentz, a tattooed Xanathar Guild enforcer. Asai pushed through the crowd to help Yagra, only to find that the fight was already nearly over. Durnan eventually dragged Yagra away from Krentz and restored order. Yagra thanked the adventurers, although she was hardly pleased that they had interfered. It was their first glimpse of the conflict that would eventually consume much more of their lives.

## Before the Paths Crossed

Before the events at the Yawning Portal brought them together, each of the adventurers had already lived a life that had shaped the person they would become. Some arrived in Gleoda searching for belonging, others carrying the weight of something they had survived, and at least one had spent much of their life believing they were destined for something greater.

### Asai Alhiem

Asai Alhiem had once belonged to a quiet family living on the outskirts of the Ardeep Forest. Her parents, Marai and Daeric, raised her alongside her elder brother Narren and younger sister Siare. Their life was modest and peaceful, shaped by the forest and the balance their community tried to maintain with it. That life ended when Asai was six. During a blood moon, her consciousness was forcibly driven from her body as Khorne took control. She could only watch as he used her hands to murder every member of her family. The experience left her hair stark white and her childhood irreversibly broken.

Asai refused to accept Khorne or the beliefs he represented. A cult devoted to him eventually took her in, convinced that she was his chosen child and would bring about a new age through the destruction of those who opposed them. They allowed her considerable freedom within their ranks, but leaving was forbidden. Asai learned to survive among people who worshipped the force that had destroyed her family, while quietly seeking ways to understand and resist it. She also developed an interest in artificing, using the resources available to her to study how she might one day help others. Eventually, the pressure of living under the cult's control became too much. She left, carrying the memories of her family, the influence of Khorne and the question of whether she could ever truly separate herself from what had happened. Her travels brought her to Gleoda and, eventually, to the Yawning Portal. There, she recognised Yagra Strongfist, an adventurer she had encountered during her travels, and settled in for what she hoped would be an ordinary evening.

### Yogen

Yogen had grown up in a remote mountain temple devoted to a draconic deity. When he was young, the temple's grandmaster received a vision naming him as a prodigy. The prophecy became the centre of Yogen's upbringing. He was told that draconic power would one day awaken within him, bringing signs such as scales, wings and the ability to breathe fire. The temple treated him as someone extraordinary long before he had done anything to earn such a reputation. That attention also left him isolated, and by his early twenties the promised abilities had still barely appeared. Eventually, the grandmaster sent him into the wider world, hoping that experience would awaken what years of temple training had not. Yogen left believing that his purpose was to prove the prophecy right, and that his worth depended on becoming the person everyone had told him he would be.

### Kami Icewood

Kami Icewood's childhood had been spent in Thornhold, a small mountain village hidden among dense greenery and tall forests. Their parents showed little interest in them, favouring their brother Teegan instead. Kami had not even been given a name at birth. Teegan was the first to call them Kami, and they later adopted the family name Icewood in the hope that it might help them feel part of the family. Eventually, they left the village, leaving Teegan a letter explaining that they had never felt they belonged and intended to find somewhere they could.

Their travels brought them to a clerical school in Gleoda, where they trained in the healing arts and placed their faith in Bahamut. Their final examination took them to a warzone, where they were assigned to a medical station behind the front lines. Kami spent every moment trying to keep the wounded alive, exhausting their magic until it began to fail. Soldiers died despite everything they could do, and the school judged the examination a failure. Rather than helping them understand the limits of their abilities, their instructors forcibly reassigned them to a tradition devoted to death and redirected their faith towards Myrkul.

The change proved more successful in practical terms. Kami learned to work with death, resurrection and temporary undead, even raising fallen soldiers to fight again long enough to give their side another chance. They passed the second examination and left the battlefield with someone they had come to know as Whiskey, a quiet, armoured companion who followed them wherever they went. Kami was barely twenty-one by human reckoning and had no clear home to return to. They began performing in taverns and on the streets for coin, travelling on Whiskey's shoulder and using a long pole to climb up and down from the great armour. Whiskey carried a greatsword with a conspicuous crack through its blade.

The two became a familiar sight. To most people, Whiskey appeared to be an enchanted suit of armour directed by a travelling cleric. Kami rarely explained their companion, and the questions that did arise were easy to avoid. Yet the memories of the battlefield followed them. They had wanted to be a healer, but remembered the people who had died under their care. Their skill with death magic had given them another way to serve, though it had not answered the question that troubled them most: whether they could ever become the healer they had first set out to be.

### Hylander

Hylander had spent most of their life travelling with a circus. It was the closest thing they had to a home, and their father was a central part of that life. Everything changed when the group came across a strange mask on the road. After Hylander's father put it on, shadows gathered around him and took control of his body. Desperate to get their father back, Hylander challenged the force behind the mask to a game and gambled away their own freedom. They won, freeing their father, but the terms of the bargain required Hylander to wear the mask as their new ally. The entity called itself Mask, the Lord of Shadows. The arrangement proved to benefit them both, although neither could entirely separate where one ended and the other began.

Hylander's life thereafter was shaped by risk, loyalty and an unusually high willingness to gamble everything for the people they considered family. They would later reconnect with Junky, the monkey they had known from the circus, remembered for throwing rubbish at people. By the time Hylander reached Gleoda, they had already learned that family could be complicated, that bargains always carried a price and that the Mask was not simply an object they could discard. Like the others, they arrived with a past that had never quite let them go.
## Volo's Request

The violence of the Yawning Portal was followed by something far more ordinary: a missing friend. Volo approached the adventurers with a desperate request to find Floon Blagmaar. Floon had been caught in a case of mistaken identity after being taken for Renaer Neverember, the son of Lord Neverember. The search gave the party their first real reason to explore Gleoda, and every street began revealing another piece of the city's hidden conflict. The search took them from the streets into the city's criminal underworld. What looked at first like a simple kidnapping became a confrontation with the Xanathar Guild, ending with Floon rescued and the party returning to the Yawning Portal.

## A Home in Trollskull Alley

Volo could not give them the fortune he had promised. Instead, he gave them something more important. He gave them a home. Trollskull Manor was old, abandoned and haunted. In another story it might simply have been a reward, but for the party it became the first place that belonged to all of them. The building gave them somewhere to argue, recover, work and eventually return to. They quickly discovered that reopening a business in Gleoda was not as simple as repairing a few walls. The city's guild system touched everything. Carpenters, roofers, plasterers, cellarers, plumbers, launderers, street workers, butchers, brewers and bakers all had their part to play. The party took jobs, earned money and slowly rebuilt the Manor. This period mattered. They were not simply waiting for the next adventure. They were becoming residents. The Manor stopped being an abandoned building and became their place. Lif, the ghost of the former barkeeper, was part of that process. Rather than simply destroying him, the party's efforts to restore the tavern gave the spirit a reason to accept them. The Manor became a home because they treated it as one.

The next day, Asai wandered into Gleoda and found Corellon's Crown, where she met Fala Lefaliir. She bought flowers and dandelion bulbs, imagining them growing outside the abandoned Trollskull Manor that would soon become their home. The Zhentarim also approached her, impressed by what they had seen. She refused them, misunderstanding the organisation as a single unified group. The Harpers were more successful. They saw something in both Asai and Hylander and invited them to the opera, where they made their offer. They accepted. It was one of the first signs that the city was beginning to pull the party into its own web of relationships.

## The Fireball

The party slowly became the first part of Yogen's life that did not revolve around the prophecy. They argued with him, annoyed him, depended on him and treated him as a person rather than a prodigy. For the first time, he found himself caring about people who had nothing to do with his destiny.

His draconic power began to surface alongside that change. It did not awaken because he had finally proved himself worthy. It appeared as the group became more important to him, and as responsibility replaced the need to prove himself. The party was becoming something more than a collection of adventurers, and each of them was changing because of the others.

## The Sea Maidens Faire and the Stone

Asai followed the clue to the Sea Maidens Faire and uncovered Jarlaxle Baenre's involvement. Jarlaxle built the Nimblewright and used it to monitor the Gralhunds, hoping to obtain the Stone of Golorr. The party followed the trail to Gralhund Villa. What followed was not a clean investigation but the sort of chaotic operation that would become characteristic of them. 

The rest of the party infiltrated the Gralhunds Estate and recovered the Stone of Golorr. They immediately realised that carrying the stone openly was a terrible idea. Their solution was considerably more ridiculous. They took it to Steam and Steel, where the fire genasi Embric and water genasi Avi helped embed the Stone into Whiskey's chestplate. The two smiths were sworn to secrecy.
## The Cassalanter Dilemma

Only a few days later, the party received another request. Victoro and Ammalia Cassalanter wanted their help. Their children, Elzerina and Terenzio, were supposedly suffering from a curse. The Cassalanters presented themselves as desperate parents seeking help, and the party agreed to investigate. The published adventure reveals a deeper tragedy behind this story: the Cassalanters sacrificed their eldest son and desperately tried to save their remaining children through a bargain with Asmodeus. In this campaign, that material became the foundation for the party's confrontation with the family's hidden cult. On the journey back to Trollskull Manor, the party was attacked by bandits. They defeated them all except one, a firbolg named Tammith Reznisad, whom Asai dragged back to the Manor for questioning. Tammith eventually revealed the cult's location. Beneath a house in the North Ward, the party found a strange red orb. Asai recognised it as a Blood Cyst and plunged her head into it. The vision confirmed that the Cassalanters were being watched and revealed something far worse: the family intended to use the wealth and lives of the city's nobility to satisfy their pact and save their children. 
### Theo

By this point, Theo had become part of the group. Am elderly wizard whose life had already been touched by forces beyond ordinary time, asking to work for his stay in the Trollskull Manor, they carried a strange bag that could hold far more than its appearance suggested. It was an unusual tool, but one that would prove invaluable during the party's increasingly improbable plans.

The party infiltrated the Cassalanter estate by hiding inside it. Hylander disguised himself as a guard and escorted Asai inside as a supposed guest. Elzerina mistook Kami for a plush toy, and the operation grew increasingly absurd as they kidnapped guards and smuggled themselves deeper into the estate. The banquet ended in chaos. A mimic concealed among the furniture attacked, and the party pursued Ammalia upstairs. There they discovered Osvaldo, the Cassalanters' lost son, transformed into a Chain Devil and bound beneath the roof. Victoro revealed the truth. Osvaldo was their son. The creature tore down the supports holding him, and the roof collapsed. Above them, the Blood Moon appeared. Asai changed. Elzerina changed. Terenzio changed. Khorne's influence and the Cassalanters' pact twisted them into monstrous reflections of themselves. Asai was dragged back toward the same feral state that had once taken her family from her. Then the green figure appeared. He called himself Khurish.

# Part Two: Gemalon

Khurish opened a portal, and the party fell through. Before the fall, he had offered Asai something she desperately wanted: safety from Khorne. Khurish presented Gemalon as a place Khorne's reach could not touch. That promise became one reason Asai remained entangled with him, even as the party gradually realised how dangerous he was. They landed in the Forest of Tekumto, a place that looked familiar enough to be comforting and wrong enough to be terrifying. Whiskey fell from the portal and appeared to die, only to be saved by Lerissa. The incident unsettled Kami more than they let on. At times, the armour seemed to move with a will of its own, and occasional sounds came from within it that did not belong to metal. At other times, an unpleasant smell lingered around Whiskey, though Kami quickly dismissed questions about it. The party ran into the forest and fought their way through dryads before reaching civilisation. From there, the world began to unravel. They met Renair, who could no longer remember important parts of his own life. They met Quagmire, watched Quag become a puppet, encountered Lif in a basement and somehow saw him sent to heaven. They visited Cherry Grove, discussed Theo's Gin project and tried to sleep. The next day they found themselves inside Theo's bag fighting the Bagman, only for a giant frog to eat the creature. They rescued Kami and eventually discovered that Volo had been carrying them. Volo brought them to Langdale, where they found a magical version of the Yawning Portal. Durnan was old. The well was boarded shut. Yagra was still there. Something was wrong with time. Khurish had apparently spent years spying on people and terrorising cities. Aldraud was said to have lost its people. Puppets were appearing. Khurish changed forms. Even Durnan became one of his creations. The party had crossed into a world where the rules they understood no longer applied.

## The Sins

Gemalon's dangers became increasingly personal. The party was forced to confront their own sins and eventually the seven figures who embodied them. Wrath, Sloth, Pride, Envy, Greed, Gluttony and Lust were not merely enemies to defeat. They reflected things the characters carried inside themselves.

## The Dragon Attack

A solar dragon eventually broke through Gemalon's city shield. Seventy-seven people died in the attack before the party could stop it. What followed was one of the campaign's most memorable battles. The dragon filled the battlefield with fire, but the party refused to give it the victory it expected. At one point, Yogen launched himself into a backflip and kicked a ring of fireballs directly into the dragon's open mouth, turning the creature's own attack against it. The party finally brought the solar dragon down, harvested what remained of it and received gifts from Jarlaxle in recognition of what they had done. Asai kept the dragon's materials and began turning them into increasingly ambitious equipment, including the foundations of her solar dragon armour and the Steel Defender Gin. Manshoon appeared soon afterwards, while Khurish continued manipulating events from the shadows and trying to use the Stone of Gemalon to free himself.

The attack changed the group's understanding of the danger around them. Their enemies were no longer confined to hidden cults and criminal organisations. Gemalon itself was becoming a battlefield, and the consequences of their choices grew with every encounter. The solar dragon was the first time Yogen truly understood what his growing power might attract. Seventy-seven people died before the creature was stopped. The party survived, but Yogen could not stop thinking about what would happen if another dragon followed the same trail. His fear was not only fear for himself. It was the realisation that staying beside the people he loved might make them targets. Eventually the party learned that Khurish was not merely another villain. He was manipulating the conflict between the gods and Gemalon itself. The gods were leaking into the world, reality was becoming unstable, and Khurish needed the party to free him. They eventually killed Manshoon and released Khurish's soul.

## The Choice to Leave

After Manshoon was defeated and Khurish's soul was released, the group began to fracture under the pressure that had been building around them. Yogen chose to leave, convincing himself that his growing draconic power had made the others targets and that removing himself was the safest way to protect them. It was also a decision shaped by the pride that had followed him since childhood. He still believed that some problems could only be solved alone.

## An Unwanted Refuge

Away from the others, Yogen found an isolated tower overlooking a small cluster of mountain villages. He made it his home and began training with a desperation that had little to do with the prophecy anymore. He wanted control. He wanted to understand exactly what his body was becoming, and he wanted to be certain that if another dragon came for him, nobody else would pay the price.

The first weeks were almost entirely physical. He trained until exhaustion, tested the limits of his draconic abilities and repeated the forms he had learned at the temple until they became instinct. Yet the tower gave him something the temple never had. No one was there to praise him. Nobody cared that a grandmaster had once called him a prodigy. Nobody expected greatness from him. ## A New Thread in the Story

During this period, Portence entered the story. She carried the same draconic inheritance that ran through Yogen, but she also carried the arrogance and impatience that had once defined him. She wanted to be powerful immediately and treated confidence as proof that she was ready. The resemblance between them was obvious, and it quickly turned their relationship into more than simple training.

Yogen corrected her stance, taught her how to control her breathing and forced her to repeat techniques until she understood why they worked. The training gradually became a lesson in restraint, responsibility and the difference between having power and knowing what to do with it.

## The Imitation

While Yogen lived at the tower, the Sin of Envy took his place among the party. It copied his appearance and mannerisms closely enough to survive months of suspicion. The deception worked because Gemalon had already taught the party that their memories could not always be trusted. Small inconsistencies were dismissed as another symptom of the world they were trapped inside. The real Yogen never knew how much the party had suffered under the imitation. He continued protecting the villages and training Portence, believing that remaining away was still the safest choice.

## The Breaking of Time

The Gluttony Ring became the centre of a new crisis. The party travelled to Fyllanore with Renaer, hoping to build a force capable of opposing the Seven Sins. There they confronted the King of Fyllanore, who was revealed to be the current wielder of the Gluttony Ring. The fight tore through the strange city and up into the King's castle in the trees. The party fought him back through the halls and onto the heights of the castle, finally overwhelming him and throwing him from the treetop fortress to his death. When the fighting ended, they recovered the Gluttony Ring. Only after the King was dead and the Ring was in their hands did Renaer reveal the truth. He was not Renaer at all. He was Khurish. The betrayal turned what should have been a victory into another trap. Renaer led them towards an enormous orb capable of taking people outside ordinary reality, and the party was forced through it. The portal did not carry them safely away. It threw them forty-five years into the past, where the Gluttony Ring had somehow appeared on Theo's parents' farm decades before it should have existed. The Ring had its own impossible history, forged through forces that did not obey ordinary chronology. The party eventually discovered that an owlbear had swallowed it, forcing them to hunt the creature down and fight it to retrieve the Ring from inside its body. Breaking time became more dangerous as the party fought through the distorted past. Theo eventually understood the terrible truth. To restore the history they knew, he had to kill his own parents, Olivia Armadeus and Mrs Armadeus. There was no clean choice. He could save his parents and let history remain broken, or he could make the sacrifice that would restore the lives of everyone who depended on the timeline staying intact. Theo made the choice. He killed his parents, corrected the timeline and sent the party back towards Gemalon. It was one of the most painful moments of the campaign because he had no victory in it. His parents were simply gone, and everyone else could continue living because he had accepted that loss. Hylander was also changed by the broken timeline. Rather than remain with the party, he slipped away into the past. He chose his own path, leaving the others with only the knowledge that he had survived the chaos and was determined to find the answers he had been searching for. The party continued without knowing when, or if, their paths would cross again. The party escaped the broken past through another portal and fell straight into the ocean.

# Part Three: The People Who Joined Them

## Beneath the Sea

### Cascade

The party surfaced in an underwater settlement that resembled a fantastical Atlantis. The settlement had grown around a coral reef not far from the mainland, where an underwater Water Genasi community had lived for generations. Cascade had grown up among that reef, never quite accepted by the community and rarely leaving the water. On the mainland, villagers enjoyed the strange harmonies that drifted up from the sea without realising that the music came from Cascade practising alone beneath the waves. They wanted to perform for a larger audience, but believed there were always others more deserving of being heard.

When fish became scarce and the underwater village struggled to find enough food, Cascade finally ventured to the surface. There, they discovered that the music of the sea had meant more to the mainlanders than anyone had realised. As the familiar sounds disappeared, people grew sadder, but they also found new inspiration and a sense of community through the absence they shared. Cascade had unknowingly shaped the lives of people who had never known their name.

By the time the party arrived, Cascade had become involved with Khurish. They captured the adventurers and sent them to prison, setting in motion a betrayal whose true reasons would only become clear much later. For the moment, the party had more immediate problems than understanding why they had been taken.

## Another Prisoner

### Viviane

Only fifteen minutes separated the introductions of Cascade and Viviane. When the party was dragged into their cell, Viviane was already there. She had grown up in the Blacklake district of Neverwinter alongside her sister Kaela and their friend Bonny Rascal. The three had been mischievous children, known for sneaking around and causing small problems simply for the fun of it. Kaela taught Viviane to fight with two rapiers, and Viviane adopted both her sister's fighting style and the tactical instincts that went with it.

When Kaela was twenty-two, she left Neverwinter to sail with a group of pirates. At first she returned often, filling Viviane's days with stories of her adventures. Over time, those visits became less frequent as Kaela's travels took her beyond the familiar seas and into other planes. Bonny followed a different route. He attended college to become a bard before setting out to explore the stars and other dimensions. Eventually, Bonny told Viviane that Kaela had gone missing during one of her journeys and had been gone for nearly a year. He also believed he had found a way to reach the stars. Not long afterwards, Bonny disappeared as well. Viviane was left with two unanswered absences and no clear direction to follow.

Her arrival in the underwater prison added another thread to an increasingly tangled story. The group had little time to understand either newcomer before events moved again.

## The Letter

Professor Orb remained with the party and gave them a letter and a pouch. It was from Hylander, written after he had chosen to disappear into the past. The letter read exactly: \> "Dear Friends, \> I understand that tensions are high after Yogen leaving the group; however, this may be selfish of me. I want to live. I went on an adventure too young, and faced terrible things that I will never forget. Hell, I'm sure neither will you all. But I want to go and find my mother; she was absent for most of my life, and I wish to find her and know what she did, leaving me in my father's care. I just hope that if I do somehow, some time, see you again, that you won't have the same reaction as when Yogen left; that is my only worry. \> Beat that sunnuva bitch Khurish, and weirdo lich? \> P.S. Kami don't steal \> P.P.S. If Kami does, Asai smack 'im \> Here's some money and the stone as well; I was planning on giving it to you all... eventually." The letter did not repair what had happened. It simply confirmed that Hylander had chosen his own path, while the others were left to continue without him. The pouch and its contents became another reminder that the people who had travelled together were beginning to scatter, reunite and change in ways none of them could predict.

## The Court Session

The party had little time to process the letter. Professor Orb had become entangled in the strange legal machinery of the underwater city, and Kami called on an old college acquaintance, Saul, to help them. The question put before the court was as absurd as the circumstances that had brought them there: was the wormhole consensual? Sussur flowers created a dead zone in the middle of the room where magic could not function. The hearing quickly became less of a trial and more of an exposure. The party learned that Annabeth, the Sin of Lust, had taken the Gluttony Ring and carried the Lust Ring. Worse, the figure who had been travelling beside them was not Yogen. The Sin of Envy had been wearing his face and travelling with the party while the real Yogen remained elsewhere. The revelation changed the meaning of the previous months. The party had been arguing with, trusting and relying upon an imitation without realising it. The discovery left them with the unsettling knowledge that even their memories of one another could no longer be taken for granted. The courtroom did not remain orderly for long. With Professor Orb's help, the party recovered the Gluttony Ring and the Envy Ring and fled into the streets as Annabeth and the other Sins gave chase. Portence was nearby. She had travelled down from the mountain after hearing that something was happening in the city, still carrying the confidence of someone who had only recently begun testing Yogen's teachings away from his tower. She turned a corner and almost walked directly into the fleeing party. For Portence, it was an accidental reunion. For the party, it was a stranger who seemed strangely familiar. They had no idea yet that she was Yogen's apprentice and half-sister, but when the fight spilt into the street, she stepped in without hesitation. Her instincts were already those of someone trained to protect others. Annabeth attacked in the confusion, seizing Kami and trying to escape with the Gluttony Ring. Portence helped create an opening for the party to pursue her. By then, the mystery surrounding Whiskey had become impossible to ignore. The occasional sounds from inside the armour, its increasingly foul smell and Kami's growing anxiety had all pointed towards something more than an enchanted suit. The truth was finally uncovered: Whiskey was an undead being contained within the great armour, and had once been General Hisk, Kami's superior officer and a trusted friend during their time on the battlefield. The revelation gave a name and a history to the companion who had followed Kami for so long, but there was little time to reckon with it. Whiskey tried to stop Annabeth and was destroyed by her disintegration spell. Asai took his cracked greatsword, stole Annabeth's wings and struck her from the air, finally accepting her redemption oath and severing herself from Khorne. After the fight, Kami found that Whiskey had left something behind: a key and a message pointing towards a place where the party might find help. The clue led them to a hidden door and, beyond it, the Hall of the Greats. The discovery gave the party somewhere to regroup, but for Kami it was also the last thing their companion had been able to leave them. The key was not simply a way forward. It was a final act of loyalty from someone who had followed them from the battlefield to the end. Portence did not leave with the party immediately. She stayed long enough to see the aftermath, then chose to accompany them when they left the underwater city. She wanted to understand the world beyond Yogen's mountain and prove that she could use what he had taught her without him standing over her. This was the beginning of Portence's short period with the party. She was not replacing Yogen. She was carrying what he had taught her into a world he had deliberately chosen to leave behind.

# Part Four: The World Breaks

The party reached the Hall of the Greats, where Trollskull Manor had somehow survived inside a place that should not have been able to contain it. Asai used the Stone of Golorr to speak with Golorr, and the party began to understand that the Seven Sins were only one layer of the machinery surrounding Gemalon. Durnan was possessed by green fire. Khurish was found trapped in ice beneath the tavern. The party escaped towards the coast, then followed the trail of Greed into a travelling circus. Portence travelled with them, still carrying the habits Yogen had taught her even as the world around them became increasingly unstable. After the struggle, Kami finally allowed Whiskey's soul to leave the armour and pass on. They had spent so long trying to keep him beside them, first through the magic that bound him to the armour and then through sheer refusal to accept another loss. Letting him go did not erase their friendship or make the years they had travelled together any less real. It meant Whiskey no longer had to remain between life and death simply because Kami could not bear to lose him.

## The Greed Ring

Nindil Jalbuck possessed the Greed Ring. When the encounter escalated, Asai used the Envy Ring to steal the power Nindil had summoned, seized him, and flew away. She landed in the mountains of Alaquet and found a screaming girl. When Asai tried to help, the girl struck her with enough force to leave her making death saves. Portence reached Asai before it was too late. She did not stop to think about whether she was strong enough. She moved. The discipline Yogen had taught her took over before her fear could. It was one of the first moments in which the party saw that Yogen's lessons had become instinct rather than instruction.

## The Rift

Then something arrived from another universe. The Intruder threatened Gemalon itself, forcing the party into a chaotic fight at the foot of the mountain. Portence did not hang back. She went straight at the Intruder, unleashing a rapid flurry of blows and forcing it to focus on her. Then, at exactly the right moment, she ducked out of the way. The movement gave Asai the opening she needed, and Asai struck the Intruder decisively. The battle ended with the Intruder defeated, but the damage was greater than anyone realised. The walls between realities had begun to fail. Gemalon was no longer isolated. Something had changed the rules of the world, and Khurish was already moving to take advantage of it.

## A World Rewritten

Khurish reshaped Gemalon. Roads changed. Settlements were carved into different landscapes. Familiar places were moved, altered or rebuilt as though the world had been made from pieces of several different realities. More frighteningly, Khurish did not only change the land. He changed the memories of the people living within it. Most people remembered a version of Gemalon that had never existed. Friendships could be remembered differently. Journeys could disappear. People could remember having met strangers they had never seen, or forget people who had mattered to them. The party themselves were not completely protected from the changes. Portence was one of the clearest examples. She remembered being part of the party, but the details of what she had done and how she had come to know the others were no longer reliable. Across Gemalon, similar distortions appeared. The world had not simply been rearranged physically. Its history was being rewritten around the people who lived in it.

## The World Shifts

Khurish's reshaping reached Yogen's mountain. The land around the tower shifted first, and the villages he had spent months protecting were torn apart by the same changes spreading across Gemalon. The tower became an anchor point in a world that was no longer willing to remain still. Portence was caught in the same distortion. The ground beneath her gave way and the world folded around her, separating her from Yogen and the party. She was carried away with the reshaped land and eventually deposited far from the mountain, where she was taken in at a lighthouse. By the time she woke, much of what she remembered had been altered. She knew pieces of her life, but the memories of travelling with the party and the details of Yogen's training were fragmented or missing. Yogen's tower could not survive the movement. Its foundations split as the land shifted beneath it, and the structure collapsed at the foot of the mountain where the Rift had opened. The villages he had protected were scattered into the new world. The people who had known him no longer remembered all of what he had done for them. The home he had built through months of solitude was gone in moments. For Yogen, this was the moment isolation became impossible. He reached the ruins of his tower and found nothing left of the life he had built there. The place that had begun as an escape had become another piece of the broken world, forcing him back towards the people and responsibilities he had tried to leave behind. Portence was no longer there. She had been separated from Yogen during the reshaping and was now living at the lighthouse with fragmented memories. The bond between them had not disappeared completely, but neither of them could yet understand how much Khurish had taken from her. Her body still remembered lessons her mind could no longer place, and the next time she met Yogen, she would have to rediscover the connection for herself.

## A Missing Member

The party tried to establish themselves in New Gemalon. Asai continued her artificer work, Kami attuned to Gluttony, and the others tried to understand the strange new shape of the world around them. None of them had much time to settle before the next chain of events began.

## The Lighthouse

The party eventually reached a lighthouse and met its guardian, Yastok Sagurin. Portence was already there. She had lived at the lighthouse since Khurish's reshaping carried her away from the mountain, and her memories were fragmented. She remembered enough to know that the party mattered to her, but not enough to reconstruct everything she had experienced with them. She still carried Yogen's lessons in her movements, even though she could no longer remember being taught them. Seeing the party again, she instinctively told Yastok to accompany them. ### Yastok

Yastok was the first son of Damek, Elder of the Lithoids, and a member of the slave generation. He had organised the Great Rubble Movement, allowing his people to venture further into the caves of Dollodhir. Although his people needed him at home, Yastok wanted to see more of Gemalon and learn from the cultures beyond the caves. He travelled widely, sending letters back to Dollodhir with the knowledge and ideas he gathered, hoping that what he learned could help his people develop their technology and society. His letters became a way to remain connected to his responsibilities while still pursuing a life of exploration. Yastok lived with a difficult choice. His people needed him on Dollodhir, but he wanted an adventure of his own. His letters were his compromise. He could remain connected to his people while still experiencing a life beyond the caves. Portence's place in the party became increasingly complicated. Khurish had changed her memories, and she could no longer reconstruct the time she had spent with the party or the training Yogen had given her. Yet when she fought, she moved like someone trained by a patient master. The knowledge remained in her body even when the story of where it came from had been erased.

## Langdale

In Langdale, Kami pretended to be a cat while Cascade and Yastok dealt with the strange examination of Cascade's rings. A purple figure attempted to take advantage of them, only to lose a hand when the situation turned against him. Cascade and Yastok were thrown into a hole and found a strange table that wouldn't let them escape until they revealed a secret. Cascade admitted that they had lied to obtain their guard position because their parents expected them to become a general. Being a guard had seemed like the next best option. Yastok had begun to develop an awkward affection for Cascade, and what started as a joke became a shy relationship between two people trying to figure out what they wanted from a life others had already planned for them.

## The Descent

The party eventually found themselves in the Undermountain. They ran from minotaurs, fought drow and found a tiger. Gemalon continued folding familiar dangers into unfamiliar places.

## The Facility

The party infiltrated a facility in the Undermountain and were drawn into a gruelling fight. Kami fought desperately to keep everyone alive, drawing on everything they had learned from both traditions of their training. Their healing magic was no longer simply an attempt to undo death after it happened. They used it to keep their companions standing, buying the others time to find a way through the facility. Yastok attempted to sacrifice himself in Yogen's place, but the effort failed. Yogen was disintegrated, leaving the group facing yet another loss in the middle of a battle they had barely survived. The encounter forced Portence to confront how quickly confidence could become recklessness. She survived because she had learned to trust the people around her, even though she could no longer remember exactly when Yogen had taught her to. In the aftermath, Yastok gave Cascade his childhood luck stone.

## A Plan to Save Gemalon

The party began planning how to save Gemalon. Professor Orb and Leo Fastus joined the growing group around them. Khurish appeared in the basement and offered Cascade a bargain, claiming that releasing him would save Cascade and Yastok. The party had to consider whether killing Khurish would destroy the world they had been trying to save. Yastok proposed imprisoning Khurish inside a new stone, built on the principle of the Stone of Gemalon and powered by the Rings and forces from beyond the world. It was not a clean victory, but it offered the possibility of preserving Gemalon without allowing Khurish to continue controlling it.

## A Broken Message

Cascade sent Asai a Sending. Her response came back fragmented: cave, jail, shackles, underground, ritual, blood, Khorne, cult, help. Asai answered with the words that mattered most: freedom, fate, destiny, reject, brand, control, war, invasion. The Ring was still safe, but she knew she would have to fight.

## Nazri

In Nazri, the party lost time drinking with Al the alcoholic, chased a dead-end artificer and eventually spoke with Olidammara. Even this encounter concealed another layer of Khurish. The Parasite and Mammon had become entangled, and another strange circus began to form around them.

## The History Beneath the World

At last the party learned the history behind the world. Hundreds of thousands of years ago, a Creator looked across the cosmos, fascinated by Toril but unable to enter it. The Creator created Gemalon as a playground and created an Archivist to catalogue the people taken from other worlds. That Archivist was named Khurish Elanil Vagan-Exyll. The gods of the world from which those people had been taken eventually became enraged. They struck down the Creator's puppet and split Khurish into four aspects: the Parasite, carrying creativity and amusement; the Ancient, carrying anger and villainy; the Archivist, carrying secrecy and wickedness; and the Lost, carrying what remained of his goodness. The Lost was imprisoned elsewhere, while the other aspects continued to shape Gemalon. The body was sealed behind a barrier that could only be broken by retrieving the Stone of Gemalon. Khurish had spent ages manipulating adventurers into finding it for him. Eventually, he found the specific party he believed could survive his world: the cleric balancing life and death, the sorcerer far past their time, the monk shaped by prophecy, the Aasimar marked by her past, the warlock forgotten by the world and the pirate carrying the darkest shadows.

## The Search

The party refused to leave Asai lost. They tracked the skywrite spell she had managed to cast from within her holding spell, following the message until it led them towards the cult that had taken her. The cult had been preparing a ritual designed to reverse Asai's growth and force her back towards her Khornish nature. They intended to undo the changes she had fought so hard to make. The party broke into the ritual and fought to stop it. Yastok saw Asai in danger from the ritual's effects and threw himself into the path of the power. He succeeded in protecting her, but the ritual transformed him into a mutated Blood Stone Monster. Cascade was eventually forced to drive him into a ravine. Their relationship ended in a brutal act of survival rather than a clean farewell.

## The World Begins to Break

Khurish eventually found the Rogue card from the Deck of Many Things. He claimed that the card convinced him to take a stand, overthrow the existing hierarchy and become something better. In his mind, controlling Gemalon had become indistinguishable from saving it. His attempt to impose that vision on the world became the final stage of the conflict.

He began taking people from the world and turning them to stone. Around twenty per cent of Gemalon's population was removed in this way. The party had reached the point where defeating Khurish was no longer only about escaping his world. It was about deciding what would happen to everyone born inside it. He began taking people from the world and turning them to stone. Around twenty per cent of Gemalon's population was removed in this way. The party had reached the point where defeating Khurish was no longer only about escaping his world. It was about deciding what would happen to everyone born inside it. The Breaking of Gemalon was finally set in motion by Cascade's betrayal. Cascade sold out the party to Khurish, not because they had stopped caring about the others, but because Khurish offered exactly what Cascade wanted most: Yastok restored to life and a path out of Gemalon. In exchange for delivering the party into Khurish's hands and helping force them toward the rupture, Cascade was promised that they and Yastok would be sent to the outside world, where they could live a quiet, peaceful life together. It was a selfish choice born from grief and desperation, and Cascade believed it was the only way to get Yastok back.

# Part Five: The Null Expanse

## Beyond Gemalon

## The Man Who Waited

Before the party could begin the final journey into the heart of the Expanse, they found someone they had not expected to see again. Hylander was waiting for them in the darkness. He was older now, harder around the eyes and visibly worn by years that had not passed for the others in quite the same way. The Hylander who had escaped into the past was still there beneath the scars, but he had become more cautious and far less interested in pretending that the world would give anyone a clean ending. He had survived long enough to find his way through the broken paths of time and eventually into the Null Expanse. He did not ask the party to rescue him. He had been waiting because he knew what lay ahead, and because he had learned that sometimes the most useful thing an old adventurer could do was point the younger ones toward the road, then walk it with them a little longer.

## The Way Forward

With the nine lessons understood, the Null Expanse changed. The fragments around them stopped resembling prisons and began to resemble roads. The Lost Echoes could now be heard calling from beyond the darkness, and the party understood that the Expanse had not merely been testing them. It had been trying to make them understand why the Warden's promise of perfect preservation was so dangerous. A life without loss was not a life. It was a memory that had been prevented from ending. The party continued towards the heart of the Expanse, carrying the lessons they had earned rather than the things they had lost.

## The Crownless Void

The final region belonged to none of them individually. It showed the party the world they could have created if they had never learned any of the lessons that brought them there. Asai ruled through fear. Kami refused every death. Theo tried to rewrite every loss. Yogen stood above his students. Hylander kept searching for the family he could never recover. Viviane chased every missing person until nothing remained of herself. Portence tried to become whatever others expected. Yastok sacrificed his own life to every obligation. The vision did not show evil versions of them. It showed versions who had never changed. The party finally understood that none of their struggles had been about becoming perfect. They had been about learning to live with their flaws without letting those flaws define them. The Crownless Void taught them the final lesson: they did not need to become different people. They only needed to choose who they would be.

## The Impossible City

The Impossible City was built from fragments of Yogen's teachings, but every street contradicted the last. Portence found versions of herself who were reckless, obedient, arrogant and afraid. Each claimed to be the real Portence. The city tried to convince her that without her memories of Yogen and the party, she had no idea who she was. Instead, Portence began fighting by instinct, using the lessons her body still remembered. She did not need to recover every memory to know what kind of person she wanted to become. The Impossible City taught Portence that identity is not only something remembered. It is also something chosen.

## The Crushing Dark

The Crushing Dark pressed down on Viviane with the weight of every person she had ever lost. Kaela and Bonny appeared before her, trapped beneath the darkness, and called for her to save them. Viviane tried to carry them both, but every step made the weight heavier. Eventually she realised the Expanse wasn't asking whether she loved them. It was asking whether she could accept that loving someone did not give her ownership over their fate. She set down the weight and reached for them without trying to drag them where she wanted them to go. The darkness lifted. Viviane learned that connection could survive distance and uncertainty, and that she didn't have to destroy herself to prove she cared.

## The Broken Orbit

The Broken Orbit was made from fragments of roads leading to different points in Hylander's life. One path led to his father. Another led to his mother. Another led back to the circus. Each promised that if he chose correctly, he could finally find the family he had been denied. The Mask offered to choose for him, as it had done so many times before. Hylander refused. Instead, he walked away from every road that demanded he become someone else's idea of family. He understood that his family did not have to be the people who had raised him, abandoned him or even understood him. It could be the people he chose and who chose him in return. The Broken Orbit taught Hylander that belonging is something you build, not something you inherit.

## The False Reflection

The False Reflection showed Yogen a thousand versions of himself. In each one, he was stronger, more famous and closer to the prophecy he had spent his life trying to fulfil. At the centre stood one final reflection, a version of Yogen who had become everything the temple had promised he could be, but who stood completely alone. The reflection told him that Portence would always remain beneath him, that his students would always need him and that the only way to protect them was to remain the strongest person in the room. Yogen finally turned away. He understood that becoming greater than everyone else would only recreate the loneliness that had shaped him. His purpose was not to be the greatest. It was to make sure somebody else could become great after him. The False Reflection taught Yogen that a legacy is not measured by how far above others you stand, but by how many people can stand beside you when you are gone.

## The Endless Vault

The Endless Vault was a place where nothing could ever truly be lost. Every object the party had ever carried, broken or abandoned, could be found there. Theo discovered a room containing his parents exactly as they had been before the broken timeline. They welcomed him home and offered him the chance to remain with them forever. The price was simple: leave the others behind and allow the timeline to unravel again. Theo almost accepted. Then he remembered what it had cost to correct the timeline the first time. He walked away, choosing the life he had built rather than the life he wished he could have kept. The Endless Vault taught Theo that loving what he lost did not mean he had to destroy the present to possess it again.

## The Killing Field

The Killing Field was filled with endless battles between people who could never finish dying. Kami was given the power to resurrect everyone they touched, and at first they believed this was exactly what a healer should do. But every person they restored immediately returned to the same battlefield, trapped in an endless cycle of suffering. Kami eventually stopped. They realised that healing was not the same as refusing to let someone die, and that resurrection could become another form of cruelty when it only returned someone to suffering. Whiskey's memory returned to them in that moment, reminding them of the choice they had made when they allowed him to rest. Yet the lesson was not that they had failed as a healer. They had kept their companions alive when it mattered, and the lives they had saved were no less meaningful because they could not save everyone. Kami finally understood that healing was measured not by an impossible promise to defeat death, but by the care they gave to the living. The Killing Field taught them that death could be part of mercy, without making life any less worth protecting.

## The Red Silence

The first region was almost completely silent. Every sound disappeared the moment it was made, leaving only the sight of violence without the comfort of hearing it. Asai found herself surrounded by visions of the night her family was murdered through her body. The Expanse offered her a bargain: surrender her anger and her memories, and she would never have to feel responsible for what happened again. Asai refused. She had spent years believing that freedom meant destroying the thing that controlled her. Here she finally understood that freedom also meant accepting that what had been done through her was not her choice. She could remember it without becoming it. The Red Silence taught Asai that her past could explain her, but it did not own her.

## The Lessons of the Null Expanse

The Null Expanse did not test the party simply by trying to kill them. Each region seemed to understand something about the people who entered it. The deeper they travelled, the clearer it became that the Expanse was forcing them to confront lessons they had spent the campaign avoiding. The visions belonged to individuals, but the journey belonged to all of them, because every lesson changed the way the group understood one another and the world they had crossed to reach this place.

## The Final Khurish

Deep beneath the Null Expanse, the party finally reached Khurish's final form. What waited for them was not the triumphant godlike enemy they had expected. His broken form was imprisoned below the Expanse, barely holding together, and he begged them to kill him before the other fragments could reach him. Khurish told them the others had become corrupt. If they reunited his broken form, they would not restore what had once existed. They would take Khurish's combined power and use it to make a plaything out of the world the party had fought so hard to protect. There was no grand duel. No final exchange of powers. It was closer to an execution. Khurish wanted it to end, and the party understood that allowing the other fragments to reunite him would condemn Gemalon to another cycle of manipulation. Asai stepped forward and ended it. She raised her revolver and fired a smooth, deliberate shot into the back of Khurish's head. The Expanse reacted immediately. It rejected the remaining fragments, sealed Gemalon away with itself and closed the path between them. The world was not destroyed. It was removed from reach. The Null Expanse folded over Gemalon like a final door, and from that moment onward there would be no road back.

## The Vault

The party was ejected from the Expanse and returned to the ruins of their old tavern. The place that had once been the beginning of their adventure was broken, quiet and almost unrecognisable. Among what remained was the key to the Vault they had begun searching for during the events that had started their journey. They had carried the possibility of that treasure through years of disasters, and now, with Gemalon beyond their reach and the final battle behind them, nothing was left to stop them. They went forth together and claimed their spoils. For once, the reward did not need to hide another catastrophe. It was simply theirs. It was there, surrounded by the remnants of the place where their story had begun, that Asai finally stopped pretending that the feelings she had carried for Viviane were only another part of the chaos. Their love had been burning quietly in the background for a long time, growing through every crisis, argument and moment of trust. Asai proposed to Viviane.

# Epilogue: What Remained

The world they returned to was changed, but it was alive. Kami continued without Whiskey, carrying his memory rather than his body. She understood that love was not the same as possession, and that sometimes the kindest thing a person could do was allow someone to rest. Cascade carried the memory of the sea and the culture beneath it into the wider world. The music that had once gone unheard became part of something larger, proving that isolation did not make a life meaningless. Viviane continued searching for Kaela and Bonny, but she was no longer searching alone. Whatever remained unresolved, she had people beside her. Hylander found the freedom he had wanted. He had left the party and escaped into the past, but his story did not end there. He found his mother, learned the truth he had spent years searching for and eventually built a life that was his own. The Mask remained with him, not as a chain but as a reminder of the choices that had carried him forward. Asai returned to the question that had haunted her since childhood. She was not responsible for what Khorne had done through her body when she had no control. She was responsible for what she chose to do afterwards. She chose herself.

## He Was Never Meant to Be the Greatest

Yogen had spent his youth believing that being extraordinary was the purpose of his life. By the end, he understood the opposite. His greatest accomplishment was not becoming the strongest person his prophecy had promised. It was making sure the people who came after him would have someone to learn from. He was meant to be the first, never the greatest. Yogen survived. He returned to the mountain temple with Portence beside him, not as the boy who had once left desperate to prove a prophecy true, but as the teacher the prophecy had been waiting for. He continued training Portence and began teaching others, building a tradition in which every generation would pass the dragon's power and its lessons to the next. Yastok returned to the responsibility he had never stopped carrying. He continued writing to Dollodhir, sharing what he learned, while finding a way to remain an adventurer. His relationship with Cascade became one of the things that connected his two lives rather than forcing him to choose between them. The first generation of the Shattered Lands did not end with a perfect victory. It ended with people who had survived long enough to become something more than the people they had been when they first entered the Yawning Portal.

## Source and Rewrite Notes

The supplied campaign notes are the authority for the chronology and player-driven events. The character documents are used to reconstruct each adventurer's history, motivations and intended development, while preserving the events that occurred during the campaign. Waterdeep: Dragon Heist is used only as structural reference for the opening, with Gleoda replacing Waterdeep in the Shattered Lands. The campaign has been rewritten as a continuous history rather than a session guide. Where the notes contain contradictions or apparent deaths, this version follows the established rewrite direction and the intended character arcs, including Yogen's survival and his eventual return to the mountain temple. The original Hell material has been translated into the Null Expanse, a cosmic space between realities. Infernal geography and terminology are therefore not part of the Shattered Lands canon. The Breaking of Time now records Olivia Armadeus and Mrs Armadeus. Hylander is the author of the letter delivered by Professor Orb, and the letter is reproduced exactly as supplied by the campaign notes.
